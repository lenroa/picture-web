No Priors - Pax Silica: Inside the Trump Administration's Tech Strategy with US Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg

The Takeaway: Jacob Helberg's AI supply-chain strategy is not “government builds everything,” but “government creates trusted zones and legal frameworks so private companies can build commercially viable supply-chain platforms.”

Jacob Helberg, US Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs, frames Pax Silica as a 14-country economic-security coalition for the AI supply chain. The concrete example is a 4,000-acre economic security zone in the Philippines, described as a forward-deployed industrial base that combines US legal predictability with Philippine manufacturing strengths. His sharpest point is that AI supply chains are not just chips: they include precision reducers, servo motors, rare earth magnets, actuators, logistics, electricians, data-center inputs, and robotics components.

Helberg contrasts this with China's Belt and Road model. His criticism is that state-owned enterprises and centrally allocated capital can create waste, overpricing, debt traps, and political leverage. The US alternative he describes is a product-centric foreign policy: work with builders and private companies, make the platform commercially viable, and let it live outside government as a private service. One memorable line: “We're not gonna do government operated supply chains because that's not how we shine as a country.”

For AI builders, the practical signal is that supply-chain security is becoming a product surface, not just a policy memo. If robotics, data centers, energy, minerals, logistics, and manufacturing all become constraints on AI scaling, then the next wave of AI infrastructure companies may look much more industrial than software people are used to.
